## PROMPT 1: The Kurgan Seal

"In the style of a 19th-century antiquarian illustration: ink and sepia wash on aged parchment, fine crosshatched shadows, scholarly and precise yet subtly disturbing. Muted palette of sepia, charcoal, tarnished gold, faded sky blue, and rust red. The image appears documented rather than imagined, with water stains, foxing, and marginal notes on damaged paper. Wrongness emerges through details, not overt horror.

A detailed archaeological sketch of a Scythian gold pectoral or medallion, rendered with obsessive precision. The central motif depicts a stag in the traditional animal style, its antlers branching into elaborate spirals. Upon close inspection, the spiral patterns fail to resolve geometrically, and within the antler loops hang tiny human figures like wheat sheaves at harvest. The stag's eye is rendered as pure black void with faint pinprick stars within. Measurement annotations in faded ink along the margins. A handwritten note reads: 'Kurgan 7, lower chamber. The diggers would not touch it.' Tarnished gold coloring with traces of green patina and rust-colored residue in the deeper engravings."

## PROMPT 2: The Sky That Watches

"In the style of a 19th-century antiquarian illustration: ink and sepia wash on aged parchment, fine crosshatched shadows, scholarly and precise yet subtly disturbing. Muted palette of sepia, charcoal, tarnished gold, faded sky blue, and rust red. The image appears documented rather than imagined, with water stains, foxing, and marginal notes on damaged paper. Wrongness emerges through details, not overt horror.

A top-down technical illustration of a Tengrist shaman's drum, the stretched hide surface mapped with ceremonial symbols. The center depicts the Eternal Blue Sky as a great circular void in faded blue-grey wash, but the rendering suggests depth—and within that depth, the faint impression of a half-lidded eye looking downward. Around the drum's rim, simplified mounted figures ride in procession, but their cast shadows bend toward the center regardless of any light source. The hide texture is rendered with careful crosshatching, showing wear and age. Scattered notes in the margins attempt to decode the symbols, growing increasingly cramped and agitated. A water stain near the bottom edge suggests the shape of a hand reaching upward."

Muted palette of sepia, charcoal, tarnished gold, faded sky blue, and rust red. The image appears documented rather than imagined, with water stains, foxing, and marginal notes on damaged paper. A rubbing or careful sketch of a stone altar surface, documented as if for an archaeological journal. The carved symbols blend Scythian spiral motifs with angular proto-Mongolian script. At the composition's center, a curved blade forms a crescent—annotated tentatively as 'ceremonial? agricultural?'—surrounded by carved stalks that the marginal notes first identify as wheat but then correct with an unsteady hand: 'Not wheat. Vertebrae. Spinal columns arranged as grain.' The carving style suggests great age, worn by centuries. A measurement scale is drawn at the image edge. Foxing and age spots scatter across the parchment, and one brown stain near the blade symbol has no annotation at all."

## PROMPT 4: The Rider Between Stars

"In the style of a 19th-century antiquarian illustration: ink and sepia wash on aged parchment, fine crosshatched shadows, scholarly and precise yet subtly disturbing. Muted palette of sepia, charcoal, tarnished gold, faded sky blue, and rust red. The image appears documented rather than imagined, with water stains, foxing, and marginal notes on damaged paper. Wrongness emerges through details, not overt horror.

A field sketch of a petroglyph discovered on a standing stone, rendered in careful ink lines against a wash suggesting dark rock. The ancient carving depicts a mounted rider holding a long curved implement—scythe or shepherd's crook, the notes cannot determine. The horse beneath the rider possesses too many legs, though the scholarly annotation attempts to explain this as 'artistic convention or damage to stone surface.' Above the rider, a constellation pattern is mapped with dotted lines, but a troubled footnote observes that the stars match no known celestial configuration, unless certain familiar stars have been rearranged into a new pattern suggesting a reaping motion. The parchment is torn at one corner. Faint pencil marks attempt to calculate dates and alignments, then stop abruptly."

## PROMPT 5: The Treaty No One Signed

"In the style of a 19th-century antiquarian illustration: ink and sepia wash on aged parchment, fine crosshatched shadows, scholarly and precise yet subtly disturbing. Muted palette of sepia, charcoal, tarnished gold, faded sky blue, and rust red. The image appears documented rather than imagined, with water stains, foxing, and marginal notes on damaged paper. Wrongness emerges through details, not overt horror.

A reproduction sketch of a fragmented diplomatic document, rendered as if catalogued by a museum archivist. The central focus is a wax seal, half depicting the Papal crossed keys, half showing a Mongol tamga, but where the two symbols merge they dissolve into older knotwork—Scythian in origin, yet forming patterns that resist the eye's attempt to follow them. The surrounding parchment shows fragments of text in Latin, Mongolian script, and a third language the annotations cannot identify. Marginal translations grow increasingly frantic, with words scratched out and rewritten. Water damage along the document's edge forms stains resembling fingers or perhaps roots. A final note in different handwriting states simply: 'This document is not in the Vatican archives. It cannot be. I have checked three times.'"

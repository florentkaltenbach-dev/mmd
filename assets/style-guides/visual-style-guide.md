# MURDER MYSTERY STYLE GUIDE: THE HARVESTER'S SKY

## ARTISTIC MEDIUM

Render all images as aged scholarly illustrations, as if drawn by a 19th-century antiquarian who discovered something he should not have. The style blends archaeological documentation with creeping wrongness. Think of etchings from forbidden academic texts—precise, obsessive linework that slowly reveals disturbing details.

**Primary technique:** Ink and sepia wash on aged parchment, with selective touches of muted color. Crosshatching for shadows. Fine pen lines for detail. The overall impression should be of something found pressed between the pages of a journal that ended mid-sentence.

---

## COLOR PALETTE

### Dominant Tones
- Sepia
- Burnt umber
- Parchment cream
- Charcoal black

### Accent Colors (used sparingly)
| Color | Usage |
|-------|-------|
| Tarnished gold | Scythian metalwork, sacred objects |
| Deep sky blue (edging toward grey) | Tengri, the watching sky |
| Oxidized copper green | Ancient patina, decay |
| Rust red | Old blood, ochre pigments, faded ceremonial marks |

> **Color should feel faded, as if the image itself is ancient. Bright tones are forbidden. Everything carries the weight of centuries.**

---

## MOOD AND ATMOSPHERE

**Scholarly dread.**

The images should feel like evidence—calm, documented, yet deeply unsettling upon examination. Avoid obvious horror. The wrongness should creep in through details:
- Geometry that doesn't resolve
- Shadows that fall incorrectly
- Patterns that suggest meaning just beyond comprehension

*The perspective is that of someone who studied these objects too long and began to understand too much.*

---

## TEXTURE AND AGING

All images should appear on damaged parchment or aged paper:

- Fox spots and water stains (occasionally forming subtle, unintentional shapes)
- Creases and fold marks
- Edges darkened or torn
- Faded areas where time has worn the ink
- Occasional marginalia—small notes in cramped handwriting, sometimes scratched out

---

## SYMBOLIC VISUAL MOTIFS

Incorporate these recurring elements across all images to create visual cohesion:

### The Wrong Curve
Scythes, crescent moons, curved blades, and antler arcs—all share a similar sickle geometry that repeats throughout

### The Watching Sky
Empty spaces, voids, or circular forms that suggest observation—an eye implied but never fully shown

### Harvest Imagery Corrupted
Wheat, grain, and pastoral symbols that transform into spines, bones, or hanging figures upon inspection

### Animal Style Distortion
Creatures rendered in Scythian zoomorphic tradition, but with:
- Limbs that multiply
- Bodies that coil impossibly
- Eyes that number incorrectly

### The Circle of Riders
Small mounted figures circling borders or frames, sometimes upside down, sometimes facing inward

---

## COMPOSITION PRINCIPLES

1. Central subject rendered with scholarly precision
2. Borders or frames incorporating nomadic knotwork patterns that don't quite repeat correctly
3. Negative space that feels inhabited
4. Scale references (measurement marks, human hands, coins) to ground the fantastical in documentary realism
5. Layering: foreground detail crisp, background elements fading as if half-erased or emerging from the page

---

## WHAT TO AVOID

- Modern horror aesthetics (no dripping gore, obvious monsters, shock imagery)
- Clean, digital perfection
- Bright or saturated colors
- Symmetry that resolves comfortably
- Anything that could not plausibly appear in a 19th-century expedition journal

---

## STANDARD PROMPT PREFIX

**Use this text before each specific image description:**

> "In the style of a 19th-century antiquarian illustration: ink and sepia wash on aged parchment, fine crosshatched shadows, scholarly and precise yet subtly disturbing. Muted palette of sepia, charcoal, tarnished gold, faded sky blue, and rust red. The image appears documented rather than imagined, with water stains, foxing, and marginal notes on damaged paper. Wrongness emerges through details, not overt horror."

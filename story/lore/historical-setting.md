# HISTORICAL SETTING: THE HARVESTER'S SKY

## Time Period
**1274 CE** — The Court of Ilkhan Abaqa in Tabriz

---

## I. THE MONGOL-PAPAL DIPLOMATIC ERA

### Background: The Mongol Westward Expansion

- **1227** — Genghis Khan dies before direct papal contact
- **1245-1247** — Pope Innocent IV sends Giovanni da Pian del Carpine to the Mongol court
- **1246** — Carpine arrives during enthronement of Güyük Khan (Genghis Khan's grandson)

### The Tengri Response

Güyük Khan's response to Pope Innocent IV's letters urging conversion was a theological counterpunch:
> *The Mongols rule by the mandate of Tengri (the Eternal Blue Sky). Rather than the Mongols submitting to the Pope, it is the Pope and all Christian kings who should come and pay homage to the Khan.*

---

## II. THE FRANCO-MONGOL ALLIANCE ATTEMPTS

### After the Fall of Baghdad (1258)

The Mongols sacked Baghdad, ending the Abbasid Caliphate. They pushed into Syria, creating a strange possibility: Christian Europe allying with the Mongol "heathens" against the Muslim Mamluks.

### Key Events

**1260 — Battle of Ain Jalut**
- The Mongols, for the first time, were decisively defeated by the Mamluks
- Location: Jezreel Valley (northern Israel/Palestine)
- Ancient ground. Biblical ground.
- **Story significance:** What if both armies, in their maneuvers, disturbed something beneath that ancient soil?

**1260s-1290s — Diplomatic Embassies**
- Repeated exchanges between the Ilkhanate (Mongol Persian branch) and various Popes and European kings
- Arghun Khan sent letters to Pope Nicholas IV and Philip IV of France proposing joint campaigns
- Some Mongol leaders had Christian (Nestorian) wives and mothers

## IV. THE COURT AT TABRIZ (1274)

### The Setting
The great *ger* (tent) of the Ilkhan—silk walls billowing, braziers of juniper crackling, the aroma of fermented mare's milk (*airag*) and roasted mutton.

### The Multicultural Crossroads
A strange constellation of peoples such as no Roman emperor had ever commanded:
- Nestorian priests beside Buddhist monks
- Genoese merchants arguing with Uighur scribes
- Mongol generals and Christian princesses
- Persian administrators and Silk Road traders
- Papal envoys clutching wooden crosses

### Religious Complexity
- **Tengrism** — Traditional Mongol sky worship
- **Nestorian Christianity** — Eastern Christianity, strong among Mongol nobility
- **Buddhism** — Growing influence from the east
- **Islam** — The faith of many subjects
- **Shamanism** — The old ways, never fully abandoned

---

## V. THE SCYTHIANS: HISTORICAL CONTEXT

### Timeline
- **7th century BCE - 3rd century CE** — Scythian dominance of the steppes
- Absorbed by Sarmatians, Huns, and later Turkic peoples
- By 1274, a dead civilization—but their traces remained

### What They Left Behind

**The Kurgans**
- Ancient burial mounds dotting the landscape
- Contained gold, sacrificed horses, ritually killed servants
- **Story significance:** What if the Mongol conquests disturbed something meant to stay buried?

**Herodotus's Accounts**
- Drinking from enemy skulls
- Ritual intoxication with hemp smoke in enclosed tents
- Prophecies through bundles of sticks
- Practices that already sounded almost Lovecraftian to the Greeks

### The Phonetic Echo
While "Scythian" and "scythe" aren't linguistically related, that phonetic coincidence is exactly the kind of sinister resonance that works in Lovecraftian lore—as if language itself remembers something it shouldn't.

## VII. THEMES FOR THE MURDER MYSTERY

### Surface-Level Political Intrigue
- Alliance negotiations between Rome and Karakorum
- Succession politics (always deadly among the Mongols)
- Trade route control
- Religious conversion attempts

### The Deeper Pattern
- Deaths echoing old Scythian sacrificial rites
- A golden artifact from a kurgan making its way along the Silk Road
- References in ancient texts to something called "the Reaper of the Blue Sky"
- Siege works around contested cities breaking through into older chambers

> *Someone is using the chaos of holy war as cover for something far older.*

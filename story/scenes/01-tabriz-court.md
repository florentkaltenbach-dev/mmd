# SCENE 01: THE COURT AT TABRIZ

**Location:** Tabriz, Court of the Ilkhan Abaqa
**Date:** Year of Our Lord 1274
**Type:** Opening / Establishing Scene

---

## SETTING

The silk walls of the great *ger* billowed gently, though no wind stirred outside. Braziers of juniper crackled, their smoke mingling with the aroma of fermented mare's milk and roasted mutton. The Ilkhan had not yet arrived, but his court had gathered—a strange constellation of peoples such as no Roman emperor had ever commanded.

---

## CHARACTERS PRESENT

### Brother Domenico of Tuscany
*Papal envoy*

Clutched his wooden cross and tried not to stare. He had been in Tabriz for three weeks and still could not grow accustomed to it. Nestorian priests sat beside Buddhist monks. A Genoese merchant argued prices with a Uighur scribe. And presiding over discussions of war sat a Mongol princess who had been baptized and yet still, he was told, made offerings to the Eternal Sky.

### Nayan Borokhul
*Scarred Mongol general*

A veteran whose grandfather had ridden with Genghis himself. He traced a finger across the silk map spread on the low table.

### Dokuz Khatun
*The Khan's mother, Nestorian Christian*

Her eyes, dark and deep-set, studied the map as if reading something beneath the inked rivers. Her influence at court was whispered about in Rome itself.

### Yusuf al-Kindi
*Merchant from Damascus (or so he claims)*

His presence at the Mongol court had never been fully explained. His robes were fine, his beard perfumed, his eyes the color of old bronze.

---

## DIALOGUE

**BOROKHUL:** "The Mamluks grow bolder. They raid here. Here. They test us since Ain Jalut."

**DOKUZ KHATUN:** *(murmuring)* "Ain Jalut was a wound. But not only to our pride."

**BROTHER DOMENICO:** "My masters in Rome have authorized me to discuss... coordination. His Holiness believes that a united force, striking from Egypt's flank while you press from the north—"

**BOROKHUL:** *(waving a hand)* "Yes, yes. We have heard these proposals before. Your kings talk and talk. They send letters. They do not send soldiers."

**BROTHER DOMENICO:** "The Lord Edward of England has taken the cross. He campaigns even now—"

**BOROKHUL:** "A thousand men. Perhaps two. We buried more than that at Baghdad in a single afternoon."

---

## THE INTRUSION

A soft laugh came from the shadows near the entrance. The assembly turned.

**YUSUF AL-KINDI:** *(stepping into the light)* "Forgive me, noble lords. I merely find it amusing that you speak of burying."

**DOKUZ KHATUN:** *(voice carrying an edge)* "You find death amusing, merchant?"

**YUSUF AL-KINDI:** *(settling on a cushion without being invited—no one stopped him, no one ever did)* "I find it... consistent. The land you fight over has been drinking blood since before your ancestors learned to ride horses. Canaan. Assyria. Babylon. The soil remembers."

**BOROKHUL:** "Poetic. But I deal in swords, not poetry."

**YUSUF AL-KINDI:** "Of course. Though I wonder—have your men reported what they found in the siege works outside Aleppo? The old chambers beneath the foundations?"

*A silence fell.*

*Brother Domenico noticed that Dokuz Khatun's hand had moved to a small golden pendant at her throat. It was not a cross.*

**BOROKHUL:** *(finally)* "Tunnels. Old cisterns. Nothing of consequence."

**YUSUF AL-KINDI:** *(pleasantly)* "Nothing. Only stone altars and bones arranged in patterns your shamans refused to name. Only jars of something that was not wine and not oil and not anything your men wished to open twice."

**BOROKHUL:** *(hand drifting toward his blade)* "How do you know of this?"

**YUSUF AL-KINDI:** "I am a merchant. I travel. I listen." *(His bronze eyes found Brother Domenico)* "Tell me, good friar—in your scriptures, when the Israelites entered Canaan, what were they commanded to do with the peoples they found there?"

**BROTHER DOMENICO:** *(quietly)* "Destroy them utterly. Leave nothing alive."

**YUSUF AL-KINDI:** "And did you ever wonder why such totality was demanded? What your God feared might survive?"

---

## THE WRONGNESS

The brazier flames flickered. For a moment, the smoke seemed to form shapes—riders, or perhaps harvesters, bent over curved implements.

**DOKUZ KHATUN:** *(firmly, breaking whatever spell had settled)* "The situation in Syria will be addressed through alliance and steel. Not through old wives' tales."

But her hand had not left the pendant.

And Brother Domenico noticed that where Yusuf al-Kindi sat, he cast no shadow upon the silk walls.

---

## NOTES FOR GAME DESIGN

- This scene establishes all major characters and their relationships
- Introduces the political tension (Mamluk threat, alliance negotiations)
- Plants seeds of the supernatural (Yusuf's knowledge, the Aleppo chambers, the shadowless merchant)
- Dokuz Khatun's pendant is a key clue—it connects to the Kurgan Seal artifact
- Yusuf al-Kindi may be Nyarlathotep in disguise (or one of his cult servants)

---

## POTENTIAL PLAYER ROLES

Players could take the roles of:
1. Brother Domenico — The outsider, Christian perspective
2. A general or officer serving Borokhul — Military insider
3. A servant or scribe to Dokuz Khatun — Court intrigue access
4. A merchant in Yusuf's caravan — Silk Road connections
5. A shaman summoned to interpret the Aleppo findings — Occult knowledge
